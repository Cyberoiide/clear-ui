Before I style anything, here is the structure in three versions. The wireframes are rendered above (`first-reply.html`); they are grey on purpose, the look comes after you pick.

**What I am assuming** (tell me if any is wrong, each would change the structure):

- One freelancer, using this all day, mostly on a laptop and sometimes on a phone.
- Four to eight active projects, one timer running at a time.
- An entry is a project plus an optional short note ("Checkout flow").
- Editing entries, billing rates, clients and invoices are other screens; I left them out of this one.

**The job of the screen:** start or stop the timer on a project, then see where today and this week went.

### 1. Timer bar, day timeline, week bars (recommended)

One timer bar across the top: note, project, running time, one button. Below it, today drawn as a timeline from morning to evening with a "now" line and the entries listed under it; beside it, this week as one bar per project, sorted, with the hours written next to each.

- Start: 1 click to resume the last project, 2 for another one.
- Best at: all three things you asked for on one screen, and the gaps in your day show up without reading times.
- Costs: the week is a total per project only, no per-day view. On a phone the week sits below today.

### 2. Projects are the timer

The project list is the control: click a project to start timing it, click again to stop. Each row carries its bar for the week.

- Start: 1 click for any project.
- Best at: jumping between a handful of projects all day.
- Costs: a button on every row, nowhere to say what you worked on, today is a plain list so gaps are invisible, and it stops working past about eight projects.

### 3. Week timesheet

A grid of projects by days with totals, timer on top. Today is one column.

- Start: 1 click to resume, 2 for another project.
- Best at: filling a timesheet or preparing an invoice at the end of the week.
- Costs: today's individual entries are hidden inside one cell, you read 24 numbers where four bars would do, and it needs a different layout on a phone.

**I recommend 1.** It is the only one that shows today's entries as entries and the week per project at the same time, and the timeline answers "where did my morning go" faster than a list of times does. If switching projects in one click matters more to you than notes, take 2.
