Before I build it, here are three ways to structure the dashboard. They differ in layout, not looks; the wireframes are rendered above (first-reply.html) with sample numbers.

**What I'm assuming** (say so if any is wrong):

- You open this a few times a week, mostly on a laptop, sometimes on a phone.
- "This month" means the month so far, compared with the end of last month.
- The trial funnel uses the trials that started last month, so every one of them has had time to finish.
- The one thing you would do from this screen is write to the customers who cancelled, so that is the single primary button. Everything else is for reading.

**1. One page, three answers in order (recommended)**
Revenue and what moved it, then the trial funnel, then the cancellations table, top to bottom.
- Best at: reading the month as one story in the order you asked; same layout on a phone.
- Costs: on a small laptop the cancellations table starts below the fold.

**2. Revenue first, tabs for the rest**
A 12-month revenue chart fills the first view; the funnel, the cancellations and the breakdown sit in tabs under it.
- Best at: the long revenue trend, with no scrolling.
- Costs: two of your three questions are one click away, and you can't see the funnel and the cancellations together.

**3. Three panels side by side**
One panel per question in a single view.
- Best at: a glance on a large monitor.
- Costs: the panels are narrow, so stage names are shortened and cancellation reasons and dates don't fit; on a phone it becomes direction 1 anyway.

In all three, revenue is one number with the change next to it and a breakdown that adds up to that change, the funnel is one bar per stage with its count, and cancellations are rows.

**I recommend direction 1.** You asked three questions and it answers all three without a click, and it is the only one that keeps the reason next to each cancelled customer.
